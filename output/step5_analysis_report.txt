REQUIRED ANALYSES
============================================================

1. Does PCA improve cluster separation?
   YES. Silhouette score improves from 0.1965 (Raw 28-D)
   to 0.4798 (PCA 2-D) — a 144% relative gain.
   Davies-Bouldin index drops from 2.6953 to 0.8856
   (lower is better), confirming tighter, better-separated clusters after PCA.
   The effect is strongest at 2 components and diminishes as more
   components are retained.

2. Does k-Means converge faster after PCA?
   YES. On raw 28-D data, k-Means needed 20 iterations and 2.37s.
   On PCA 2-D: 5 iterations, 1.59s.
   On PCA 5-D: 14 iterations, 1.89s.
   On PCA 10-D: 6 iterations, 1.85s.
   PCA reduces both the per-iteration cost (fewer dims) and typically
   the number of iterations needed to converge.

3. Are clusters more meaningful in lower-dimensional space?
   Measured against true signal/background labels:
     Raw 28-D: purity=0.5475, ARI=0.0076, NMI=0.0047
     PCA 2-D : purity=0.5470, ARI=0.0073, NMI=0.0046
     PCA 5-D : purity=0.5473, ARI=0.0075, NMI=0.0047
     PCA 10-D: purity=0.5473, ARI=0.0075, NMI=0.0046
   k-Means clusters on PCA data score better on internal metrics
   (silhouette, DB), meaning the clusters are more compact and separated.
   However, none of the unsupervised clusterings align strongly with the true
   Higgs signal/background labels (ARI ~ 0, NMI ~ 0). This suggests the
   natural geometric clusters in HIGGS do not correspond to the physics
   class labels — supervised learning (Project-3) will be required to
   separate signal from background.

4. How many components preserve most variance?
   Cumulative explained variance:
     2  components: 21.5%
     5  components: 37.8%
     10 components: 59.7%
     17 components reach 80% variance
     20 components reach 90% variance
     23 components reach 95% variance
   The HIGGS feature space has high intrinsic dimensionality — no single
   component dominates (PC1 only explains 14.9%). Retaining
   ~20 components captures 90% of the variance.
